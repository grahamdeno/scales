“Convictions are more dangerous enemies of truth than lies.”
Friedrich Nietzsche, Human, All Too Human

A thought you never question isn't a belief. It's a habit. Scales makes you find out which of yours hold up.

How it works

One a day. Three honest answers.

Skip

Not today. It counts, it rests a few days, and it comes back first. Skip it often enough and Scales asks why.

Reflect

Sit with it. A real line from a thinker who would push you, never a pep talk. Reply, and they answer.

Lived

You acted on it without being reminded. It leaves the deck for your record, with where it showed up.

Question it New

Doubt counts.

Not sure it's true? Scales makes the strongest honest case against it, next to your own reasons for believing it. Then you keep it, revise it, or let it go.

Letting go is a conclusion, not a failure. It goes in your Ledger with the reason you changed your mind.

The Ledger

See what you avoid.

The thoughts you keep skipping come first, so resistance shows instead of hiding. Every row carries its last five moves.

  • Skipped. A hollow mark. Three in a row and it's in Avoiding.
  • Reflected. You sat with it.
  • Caught it. You lived it out in the world, and wrote down where.
