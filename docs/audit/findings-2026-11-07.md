# UX audit findings: Scales 2.32.1 (7 November 2026)

First full run of `tests/audit/run.js`: 18 journeys on three iPhone sizes (Chromium), 26 screen states in 12
looks (319 screenshots), 30 simulated days for five kinds of user. WebKit and the iOS Simulator runs are in CI
(`UX audit` and `iPhone Simulator audit` workflows, started by hand).

## Headline

- **85/100 average.** 17 of 18 journeys pass on every phone. The core loop (open, swipe, put away, undo, write, edit)
  scores 92 to 98 and runs at the target number of moves.
- **The schedule holds up.** In 30 simulated days every check passes for every kind of user: everything already here
  is met by day 6, day-to-day overlap is 0%, avoided thoughts never take more than 5 places, a skip is never due
  sooner than 5 days, coming back after two weeks gives a normal day of 30, and nothing moved that the person did
  not move.
- **No page errors, no sideways scroll, no layout shift, no em-dashes** in 319 screen states.
- **One real defect** (a failed save goes unseen) and **one systemic accessibility gap** (tap targets and Larger
  Text). The rest is polish.

## Findings, worst first

Severity: 4 blocks a job or loses data, 3 major, 2 minor, 1 cosmetic.

### 3. A failed save is hidden, then never mentioned again
When the phone refuses a save during a swipe or pull, the "Couldn't save" message shows for a moment and is then
replaced by "Moved on" or "Put away". A flag (`warned`) stops it ever showing again that session, so every later
change is lost without a word. The message also starts with a tick (`done()` adds "✓ "), which reads as success.
Rare on an iPhone, but it is silent data loss.
**Fix:** show a failed save as a message that stays until dismissed and is not replaced by move messages; warn again
on the next failure after it is dismissed; no tick on failures. (J18)

### 3. The two smallest targets are on frequent or only paths
"Close the list" handle is 116 x 28 and **Bring back** (the only way to restore a put-away thought) is 67 x 25. This
matches the earlier complaint that small buttons are hard to hit.
**Fix:** grow the hit area with padding to 44 high; the look does not need to change.

### 2. Most word-actions are 31 to 39 points tall
Add a note, Argue with it, Undo, Say why, Write your answer, Not a tension, the All/New/Bookmarked filters, the
color picker, Not now, Delete (45 x 43). The words-not-pills style is right; the hit areas are too short.
**Fix:** one rule: word-actions get `min-height: 44px` with the extra as padding (or an invisible `::after` hit area).

### 2. Larger Text (iPhone text size) has no effect
Every font size is in px and the web view does not follow the iPhone's text size, so people who use Larger Text get
none of it. Apple's accessibility labels on the App Store will show this.
**Fix:** have the shell pass `UIContentSizeCategory` to the page as a scale, and size type with a CSS variable
(or `font: -apple-system-body` as the root size).

### 2. A tension has no visible way out
The screen shows two thoughts and one control, "Not a tension". Leaving (tap or swipe up) is not shown anywhere, and
there is no positive action (keep it, or open either thought).
**Fix:** a quiet "Close" or "↑ Done" hint like the reading screen's "↑ Next", and a tap on either thought opens it.

### 2. After putting away, the optional action shouts
In the put-away message, **Say why** is a filled pill and the brightest thing on screen; Undo, the safety net, is
plain. The "Put away" label in the same bar looks like a dimmed button. It is also the only pill on the reading
screen, against the words-for-actions rule. The set offer after the first thought uses pills too.
**Fix:** make Undo the strongest word, Say why a plain word, and the label plain text set apart from the actions.

### 2. Argue with it: one extra tap before writing
"Write your answer" is a button that reveals the field. Show the field straight away with that line as its
placeholder. (J12 costs 5 against a target of 4.)

### 2. Tensions are three taps deep
List, then the Tensions group, then the pair. With a dot already marking new ones, a new tension could open straight
from the group row when there is only one, or show the newest pair in the row itself. (J13 costs 4 against 3.)

### 1. Keep going can bring a skip back sooner than five days
In the "keen" simulated user, 26 skipped thoughts came back before 5 days through Keep going. How Scales works says a
skip comes "back in five days". Either is fine; pick one and make the words match.

### 1. Stone color picker contrast
The unselected label in the color picker is 4.33:1 (4.5 needed).

### 1. Library rows cut thoughts to one line
Fine for scanning, but search results would read better at two lines, since the point of searching is to recognise
the words.

### 1. First screen: the example looks like typed text
The example sentence is at full display size in a dim color, and Keep is hidden until typing starts. Worth watching
in the human round (do people try to keep the example, or look for a button?).

### 1. Argue answer field is 15 px
Under 16 px iOS zooms on focus in Safari. The app's viewport (`maximum-scale=1`) prevents it inside the iPhone app,
so this only matters for the browser copy.

## What the machine cannot judge

- **Feel:** the videos (one per journey) show timing, motion and the swipe thresholds at normal speed.
- **Usability:** `docs/audit/human-round.md` has the five tasks and the SUS questionnaire for Matt and later testers.
